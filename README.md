# DonorPulse — Donor Retention & Intelligence Dashboard

An end-to-end donor lifecycle analytics project that analyzes donor retention, donation behavior, donor value, and cohort performance to identify opportunities for re-engagement and long-term revenue growth.

DonorPulse combines PostgreSQL, SQL, Python, RFM segmentation, cohort analysis, and statistical testing to transform large-scale donation data into actionable business insights.

---

## Overview

Donor retention is a critical challenge for nonprofit organizations. While acquiring new donors expands the donor base, retaining existing donors can have a much larger impact on long-term donation value.

DonorPulse analyzes more than 11.3 million donation records and approximately 3.46 million unique donors to answer key business questions:

- What proportion of donors make only one donation?
- How much donation value is generated by repeat donors?
- How does donor value change with donation frequency?
- Which donor segments should be prioritized for retention?
- How does donor retention change across acquisition cohorts?
- Which lifecycle strategies could increase repeat donations?

The project focuses on turning behavioral data into practical donor retention strategies.

---

## Business Objective

The primary objective is to identify donor lifecycle patterns and determine where targeted retention efforts could have the greatest business impact.

The analysis focuses on four areas:

1. Donor retention
2. Donor lifetime value and donation frequency
3. RFM-based donor segmentation
4. Cohort retention and lifecycle behavior

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Most donors are one-time donors

Out of approximately 3.47 million unique donors:

| Donor Type | Donors | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| One-Time | 2,463,949 | 71.08% |
| Repeat | 1,002,621 | 28.92% |

The majority of donors made only one donation, making the transition from first donation to second donation an important retention opportunity.

---

### 2. Repeat donors generate the majority of donation value

Total donation value analyzed:

**$890.2 million**

Repeat donors contributed approximately:

**86.8% of total donation value**

This demonstrates that donor retention is not simply a donor-count problem. It is also a significant revenue-protection opportunity.

---

### 3. Donor value increases with donation frequency

The analysis shows a strong relationship between donation frequency and cumulative donor value.

As donors make additional donations, their average total contribution increases substantially.

This supports prioritizing strategies that encourage existing donors to make subsequent donations.

---

### 4. RFM segmentation identifies actionable donor groups

Donors were segmented using:

- Recency
- Frequency
- Monetary value

The resulting segments were:

| RFM Segment | Donors | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Potential Loyalists | 851,028 | 24.55% |
| At Risk | 708,333 | 20.43% |
| One-Time / Low Frequency | 540,021 | 15.58% |
| Champions | 481,486 | 13.89% |
| New / Promising | 449,218 | 12.96% |
| Loyal Donors | 436,484 | 12.59% |

The At Risk and One-Time / Low Frequency segments represent significant opportunities for targeted re-engagement.

---

### 5. Cohort analysis provides a lifecycle view

Donors were grouped based on the month of their first donation.

Retention was then calculated by measuring donor activity across subsequent months.

This allows retention patterns to be evaluated by acquisition cohort rather than relying only on overall retention metrics.

---

### 6. Statistical testing confirms a difference in donor-value distributions

A Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare donation-value distributions between one-time and repeat donors.

The result was statistically significant:

**p < 0.001**

This provides statistical evidence that one-time and repeat donors have different donation-value distributions.

Statistical significance was interpreted alongside the observed business impact rather than being treated as evidence of causality.
